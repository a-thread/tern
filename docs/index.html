Tern privacy policy

Last updated: 11/22/2026

Tern is a personal health tracker for steps, food and weight. This page says what it stores, where, and what you can do about it. If you're in Washington State or another US state with a consumer health data law, see Consumer health data.

What Tern stores

Depending on what you choose to track, Tern stores:

  • your email address and first name (used to sign you in and greet you),
  • your food log (what you log, the meal, and its nutrition values), any meals you mark as “nothing today”, and the meals you save (named groups of foods),
  • if you track weight: your weigh-ins (stored in pounds, shown in pounds or kilograms as you choose), and a goal weight if you set one,
  • your waypoints, rest days and settings (goals, units, reminder times),
  • if you choose to track water: each drink you log (an amount and a time), and your daily goal,
  • if you choose to track medication: the name and due time you give each one, and which days you marked it as taken. Nothing else about it, such as doses or why you take it.
  • if you choose to track mood and stress: one check-in per day, made up of two scores from 1 to 10 (mood and stress),
  • if you choose after-meal check-ins: how full you were and how you felt (each a score from 1 to 5), and a short note if you write one,
  • if you track movement: the activities you log (the kind, minutes, how hard it felt, and a distance if you give one),
  • a note on a day, if you write one.

Where it's kept

Without an account, everything stays on your phone, in Tern's own storage. Your phone encrypts it, and it's included in your phone's backup (Google on Android, iCloud on iPhone) if you have backups on. Nothing is sent to Tern.

With Tern Plus, an account keeps your log on every phone you sign in to. Before anything is uploaded, Tern asks for your explicit consent to keep this health data on its server, and nothing is uploaded unless you agree. It is stored with Supabase, a hosted database, encrypted in transit and at rest, and protected so that only your signed-in account can read or change it. Your sign-in details on the phone are kept encrypted too. Tern relies on your consent for this (Article 9(2)(a) of the GDPR, and the equivalent under Brazil's LGPD), and you can withdraw it at any time (see Your choices).

If your data is stored outside your own country, including outside the European Union, the transfer is covered by the data processing agreements Tern has with its service providers, which include the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses.

Who else handles it

Only these service providers, and only to run Tern:

  • Supabase stores your account and, with Tern Plus, your log.
  • RevenueCat checks your Tern Plus purchase with the App Store or Google Play. It receives your account ID and purchase status, not your log.
  • Open Food Facts and USDA FoodData Central receive the text you search for or the barcode you scan (see Food information).

Steps, workouts and your health app

If you connect Health Connect on Android or Apple Health on iPhone, Tern reads your step counts and, if you leave workouts on, your workouts (their type, start and length), and only to show your steps, your streak, your daily goal and your movement. This is read on your device. Tern does not upload it, does not write anything back to your health app, and does not use it for advertising or share it with anyone. You can remove Tern's access at any time in Health Connect (Android) or Settings > Health > Data Access & Devices > Tern (iPhone), and everything you have logged stays.

Notifications

Reminders are local notifications scheduled on your device. They don't go through a server, and you can turn them off in Settings.

What Tern does not do

  • It does not sell your data, show ads, or share your data with third parties for their own purposes.
  • It does not use your weight or calories to award anything. Waypoints come from showing up: reaching your step goal, logging your meals, taking a rest day.

Your choices

In Settings, under Your data (Account and data when you're signed in), you can:

  • Export my data: get a copy of everything Tern holds about you (JSON). The file isn't encrypted, so keep it somewhere you trust.
  • Withdraw consent (with Tern Plus): Tern stops syncing, keeps your log on your phone and erases your account's copy on the server. Your account and Tern Plus stay, and you can agree again later.
  • Delete my data: erase your food log, saved meals, weigh-ins, water, medication and mood history, waypoints, rest days and settings. Your login is kept. How to delete your data.
  • Delete my account: permanently delete your account and everything stored with it. See how to delete your account.

You also have the right to ask what Tern holds about you, to have it corrected, and to complain to your data protection authority (in Spain the AEPD, in Brazil the ANPD, in Ecuador the Superintendencia de Protección de Datos Personales). Write to the address under Contact.

Consumer health data

This section is Tern's consumer health data privacy policy, for Washington's My Health My Data Act and similar US state laws. Everything Tern stores is listed under What Tern stores; all of it is health data under those laws.

  • Why: only to show you your own log, goals and progress, and, with Tern Plus, to keep it on every phone you sign in to.
  • How it's collected: from what you enter, and steps and workouts read from your phone's health app if you connect it.
  • Who it's shared with: only the service providers under Who else handles it. It is never sold, and never used for advertising.
  • Consent: nothing leaves your phone for Tern's server until you agree, and you can withdraw that in Settings.
  • Your rights: export it, delete it, or withdraw consent in Settings, or write to us under Contact.

Food information

Nutrition information for packaged foods comes from Open Food Facts, used under the Open Database License, and for everyday foods from USDA FoodData Central (public domain). When you search for a food or scan a barcode, the search text or barcode number is sent to these public databases so it can be looked up. Nothing else about you or your account is sent with it, though, as with any web request, the services can see your IP address.

Camera

If you use the barcode scanner, Tern asks for camera access. The camera is used only to read the barcode on screen; nothing is recorded, photographed or saved.

Age

Tern is intended for adults 18 and over. We don't knowingly collect data from anyone under 18. If you believe someone under 18 has created an account, contact us and we'll delete it.
